> To sustain the garden, the gardener must eat, but they must not eat the seeds of next year's harvest.

---

### Section 1: Context

The practitioner here is a steward, a gardener of a small patch of the commons: a knowledge domain, a community of practice, a piece of open infrastructure. This system is alive, a delicate ecosystem of relationships, trust and shared value. It needs the steward's energy, insight and care. The steward is also a living system with needs: resources to sustain the work, to support a family, to invest in growth. Many ways to "monetise" are on offer, but most feel extractive, like strip-mining the soil the steward has promised to protect. This is a critical metabolic juncture. The question is how a steward can draw sustenance from the system served in a way that nourishes both steward and system, so that the whole ecosystem, and everyone who draws on it, thrives over the long term.

### Section 2: Problem

> **The core conflict is Sustainability vs. Stewardship.**

A steward pours life force into creating value that flows freely. To continue that work, some of the value must flow back. This creates a deep tension. The pressure for personal sustainability pushes toward conventional models: paywalls, aggressive marketing, proprietary licences. These models are designed to maximise extraction, often at the expense of the commons. They create artificial scarcity, enclose shared knowledge and damage the trust-based relationships that are the lifeblood of the community. A pure stewardship stance, on the other hand, ends in burnout. Without a sustainable practice the steward's energy is depleted, contributions become erratic and finally stop. The steward becomes a martyr to the commons, and a depleted steward cultivates nothing. Either way the community loses: through enclosure in one case, through abandonment in the other.

### Section 3: Solution

> **Therefore, value capture is designed as a semi-permeable membrane that sustains the steward while enriching the commons.**

A practice can be understood as a living cell. It needs to metabolise, to take in resources to fuel its work. A healthy cell membrane does not just block things out; it selectively admits nutrients and releases waste, keeping the cell intact while it takes part in the larger organism. A value capture model can do the same. The aim is not to build walls but to create a regenerative loop. Valuable work is offered to the world, and channels are designed through which value returns in ways that are fair, transparent and aligned with purpose.

This is not about taking a cut. It is a reciprocal flow in which the value captured is the system's investment in its own continued stewardship. Monetisation is reframed from an act of extraction into an act of metabolic health.

The design rests on a clear distinction between the freely given commons, the garden, and the value-added services the steward provides, the harvest sold at market. Its lineage is the long history of commons and their enclosure. Elinor Ostrom's study of commons governance found that shared resources endure where the rules for drawing on them are clearly bounded, fit local conditions and are shaped by the people who depend on them. A value capture model is such a rule, and it belongs in view of the community it affects.

### Section 4: Implementation

Ethical value capture is careful ecosystem design. It asks the steward to act as both steward and entrepreneur, weaving personal sustainability into the fabric of the commons.

1.  **Articulate the Core Offering:** First comes radical clarity about the distinct value provided. Which intellectual asset or service do people find indispensable? This is not the whole of the work but its concentrated, high-value part: a curated course, a strong framework, a one-to-one diagnostic session, a custom implementation.

2.  **Define the Commons and the Product:** A clear line is drawn. The commons is what is given freely to nurture the ecosystem: published writing, open-source code, participation in community forums. The product is the focused application of expertise for which payment is asked. The concepts may be free in published articles while the structured, guided path through them is a paid course. The software may be open source while priority support and integration services are offered commercially. The line is drawn so that nothing the community already holds in common is taken back behind it.

3.  **Choose a Generative Model:** The mechanism is chosen to match the steward's ethics. Common models include:
    *   **Patronage:** Members of the community who derive value support the work voluntarily, often through recurring contributions. This is a direct investment in the steward and in the continuity of the commons.
    *   **Value-for-Value:** Products are offered without a fixed price, with a clear invitation to return value according to the benefit received. This takes courage and builds considerable trust.
    *   **Freemium / Core and Custom:** A strong core tool is provided freely, and payment is asked for advanced features, customisation or the needs of large organisations. This is the classic open-source model.
    *   **Services over Software:** The knowledge or code stays free, and time and expertise are offered to help others implement it well.

4.  **Communicate with Transparency:** The model is made open. The reasons for charging for certain things are explained, along with how that revenue sustains the free work everyone benefits from. The arrangement is framed not as a transaction but as a partnership in stewarding the commons. When people understand that their contribution keeps the garden growing, many are glad to give. Where possible, the community has a voice in where the line between commons and product runs, and the steward reports back on how the revenue is used.

### Section 5: Consequences

Practising value capture ethics changes the steward's relationship with money and work. The steward is no longer only an observer or a volunteer but a professional steward, a recognised and sustained part of the ecosystem. This brings a strong sense of integrity and allows commitment to the work over the long haul. It resolves the dissonance between the desire to serve and the need to survive. The commons becomes more resilient, because its continuity no longer depends on someone's ability to work for free. It has a dedicated, funded caretaker, and the community knows how that care is paid for.

The potential decay is subtle but real. The boundary between commons and product can be misjudged, enclosing something that should stay free, and that erodes trust. The need to generate revenue, however ethically, can quietly steer the work toward more marketable topics rather than the most needed ones. The remedy is constant dialogue with the community and with the purpose of the work, recalibrating the membrane so that the flow stays healthy and the garden, above all, thrives.

### Section 6: Cognitive Era

When people work with AI agents and machine intelligence, the landscape of value capture shifts. Agents can be strong partners in this pattern. An agent can analyse a body of work to identify the assets best suited to a paid offering. It can generate teaching materials from free-form writing, lowering the cost of building structured offerings. Agents can also support value-for-value systems, suggesting contribution levels in line with engagement and making the exchange less awkward and better informed.

The threat is equally real. The ease with which AI can scrape, synthesise and repackage knowledge can devalue the very commons a steward tends. If an agent provides a good-enough summary of a lifetime's work, the incentive to support the original steward may fall, and the commons loses the person who keeps it alive. The ethical imperative is to use AI to deepen the distinctly human contribution, taste, perspective and the ability to hold a community, and to build value capture around those qualities. Communities can also respond together, through licences and norms that ask those who build on a commons to give back to it.

### Section 7: Vitality

Vitality in this pattern feels like a healthy, breathing rhythm. It looks like a steward who is energised and focused, not burned out and resentful, fully present in the community and creating freely because basic needs are met. The community, in turn, speaks of the steward's commercial offerings with pride, not suspicion, and sees them as a necessary and positive part of the ecosystem. A steady, if not spectacular, flow of revenue corresponds to the value people receive. Testimonials speak not only about the product but about the fairness of the model itself.

Decay smells of scarcity and resentment. The steward withdraws, free contributions dwindle, and more and more time goes into revenue-seeking. Paywalls creep up around previously open content. The community starts grumbling that the steward has sold out. The language shifts from "our commons" to "their product." The steward begins to see the community not as partners but as a market to be harvested. This is the sign that the membrane has become a wall and the metabolic exchange has turned into extraction. The garden is being consumed.

**Diagnostic question:** *Could the steward explain to any member of the community what is free, what is paid and why the line runs where it does, and would that member agree it is fair?*
